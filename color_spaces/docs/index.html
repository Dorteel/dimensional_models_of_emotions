Understand the geometry and controls

RGB lightness is L = (max(R,G,B) + min(R,G,B)) / 2. A constant-lightness surface is six planar triangles joined along the grayscale axis. It is not a single perpendicular plane; at L = 0.5 its outer boundary is the six primary and secondary colors.

In HSL, z = L and radius = S × (1 − |2L − 1|). Hue is the angle, with red at 0°. Radius equals chroma, not saturation alone. This idealized circular double cone is not perceptually uniform and is not a bihexcone.

Both views use identical RGB colors and linked samples. Section mode resamples the exact selected L rather than filtering a sparse cube grid. Translucent surfaces show selected lightness even when all samples are visible. Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom, and right-drag to pan. Hover reveals coordinates; click selects a color. Sliders and checkboxes also work with the keyboard. Changing controls keeps each view's camera and the selected color.
